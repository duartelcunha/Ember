# Ember 1.2.0

The first full release for Windows since 1.0.0. Installed copies of 1.0.0 are offered it by
the updater. The candidates 1.1.0-rc.1 to 1.2.0-rc.4 fold into it.

## What changed since 1.0.0

- The ember follows the cursor without drifting; project pills and the picker share its
  geometry and stay on the monitor the cursor is on. The ember comes in three sizes.
- Before pasting, Ember checks the window, the editable element, the selection range and the
  original text, and refuses when they no longer match. Protected text (code, paths, URLs,
  accented characters) survives a refine untouched.
- The clipboard is checked once more right before the paste. If another app changed it in
  between, nothing is pasted and Ember says so.
- Terminals: Ember never types into a terminal. The result goes to your clipboard, flattened
  to one line, and you paste it yourself. Your previous clipboard content is replaced.
- Personalization imports the files you choose and shows what it kept; nothing is loaded from
  disk on its own during a refine.
- A Reply mode that answers the message you selected, on a shortcut of its own. Every settings
  tab fits the window, the window remembers where you left it, and there is a tray menu.
- Results stay in memory by default. Retained results, if you turn them on, are encrypted with
  a key in the Windows vault; turning them off deletes them. Credentials are bound to the
  endpoint they were given for.

## Installing

For an existing installation run the installer with `/UPDATE` (or `/S /UPDATE` for a silent
upgrade). Do not uninstall the old version first: older uninstallers can remove configuration
and credentials. Back up `%APPDATA%\com.deleg8lab.ember\config.json` before upgrading. The
installer carries Ember's updater signature but no Windows publisher certificate, so
SmartScreen may warn on first run.

## Known limits

- Windows only. macOS is the next track; Linux follows.
- The evidence for this release comes from one machine with one monitor at 100% scaling.
  Mixed DPI and multi-monitor setups are not yet qualified.
- Editors that do not expose their text selection to accessibility may be refused; nothing is
  pasted in that case and the result stays available for the next shortcut on the same text.
- Terminals get the result on the clipboard, one line, never typed in.

What is proven and what is not, in detail:
